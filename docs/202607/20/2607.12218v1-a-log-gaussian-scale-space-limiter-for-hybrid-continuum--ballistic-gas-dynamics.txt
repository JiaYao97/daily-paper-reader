# A Log-Gaussian Scale-Space Limiter for Hybrid Continuum–Ballistic Gas Dynamics 

Bjørn Wu 11Schraelbert Research, Oslo, Norway 

Abstract 

Rarefied hypersonic gas flows involve a continuous competition between collisional relax-ation and ballistic molecular transport. Classical Chapman–Enskog hydrodynamics is valid when the local Knudsen number is small, whereas free-molecular or kinetic descriptions are required when the molecular mean free path becomes comparable to or larger than the macroscopic variation length. Existing multiscale kinetic schemes provide robust treatments across these regimes but may be expensive when embedded in large-scale continuum solvers. In this work, we propose a log-Gaussian scale-space limiter for hybrid continuum–ballistic gas dynamics. The local Knudsen number is interpreted as a stochastic scale ratio, and the transition between collisional and ballistic transport is modeled as a probability partition in logarithmic Knudsen space. The resulting continuum and ballistic weights are com-plementary Gaussian cumulative probabilities. This construction provides super-algebraic suppression of inverse-Knudsen corrections in the continuum limit and of Chapman–Enskog corrections in the free-molecular limit. A conservative interface flux is then built by blend-ing a Navier–Stokes–Fourier flux with a half-range Maxwellian kinetic flux. The proposed framework preserves the conservative structure of the macroscopic equations, recovers the continuum and free-molecular limits, and provides a physically interpretable transition mech-anism for high-Mach rarefied flows. The method is intended as a lightweight hybrid closure suitable for finite-volume, discrete Boltzmann, or gas-kinetic solvers. Numerical tests using one-dimensional DVM/BGK Fourier and Couette benchmarks show that rarefaction correc-tions improve the macroscopic profiles relative to NSF. Calibration against these references suggests a broader transition, K0 = 0 .03 , σ = 2 .5, reducing the combined mean profile error by about 40% for the tested planar wall-bounded flows. 

Keywords: rarefied gas dynamics; Knudsen number; Chapman–Enskog expansion; free-molecular flow; hybrid kinetic-continuum method; log-Gaussian limiter; hypersonic flow. 

# 1 Introduction 

Gas flows in hypersonic and high-altitude environments frequently operate outside the strict continuum regime. In such cases, the molecular mean free path may become comparable to the macroscopic gradient length, and the local Knudsen number, 

Kn = λL , (1) is no longer small. The Navier–Stokes–Fourier equations, derived from the first-order Chapman– Enskog expansion, then lose accuracy because the distribution function is no longer a small perturbation around the local Maxwellian. In the opposite limit, when Kn ≫ 1, intermolecular collisions are weak over a macroscopic variation length and the flow approaches the free-molecular or ballistic transport regime. 1arXiv:2607.12218v1 [math.NA] 13 Jul 2026 Several families of methods have been developed to handle this multiscale problem. Direct simulation Monte Carlo is reliable in the rarefied and free-molecular regimes but becomes costly in near-continuum flows. Unified gas-kinetic schemes and discrete unified gas-kinetic schemes construct interface fluxes from kinetic evolution solutions and possess asymptotic-preserving properties across a broad range of Knudsen numbers. Moment methods, such as regularized 13-moment and higher-order systems, extend hydrodynamics by evolving stress and heat flux as independent non-equilibrium variables. Hybrid continuum-kinetic methods couple Navier– Stokes solvers with kinetic solvers based on local rarefaction indicators. The present work proposes a complementary route. Rather than introducing a full kinetic solver everywhere, we construct a physically interpretable Knudsen-dependent probability weight that blends continuum and ballistic fluxes at the interface level. The key idea is to view the local Knudsen number not merely as a deterministic switch but as a scale-space random variable. Since Kn = λ/L is a ratio of positive scales, and both λ and L may vary multiplicatively in shock layers, boundary layers, and expansion regions, the logarithm of the local rarefaction measure is a natural variable. We therefore define a Gaussian distribution in s = ln Kn and obtain continuum and ballistic weights as complementary cumulative probabilities. The main contributions of this paper are: 1. A log-Gaussian scale-space interpretation of the continuum–ballistic transition. 2. A pair of complementary Knudsen weights with correct asymptotic behavior in both 

Kn → 0 and Kn → ∞ limits. 3. A conservative hybrid interface flux combining Navier–Stokes–Fourier and half-range Maxwellian free-molecular fluxes. 4. A macroscopic equation system with Knudsen-weighted stress and heat-flux closures. 5. A DVM/BGK-based numerical assessment for planar Fourier and Couette benchmarks, including calibration of K0 and σ.

# 13 Discussion 

The proposed limiter differs from a purely empirical switch because the weights are defined in logarithmic Knudsen space and can be interpreted as a probability partition between collisional and ballistic transport. The DVM/BGK Fourier and Couette benchmarks show that rarefaction corrections improve macroscopic profiles relative to NSF in planar wall-bounded flows. The calibrated pair K0 = 0 .03 , σ = 2 .5 performs better than the default K0 = 0 .1, σ = 1 .0

for the present benchmark set. This suggests that wall-induced kinetic effects can become visible before the conventional Kn = 0 .1 threshold and that a broader transition in logarithmic Knudsen space is beneficial for these flows. The calibrated parameters should not be interpreted as universal constants. Strong shocks, hypersonic blunt-body flows, highly non-Maxwellian distributions, and multidimensional sepa-rated flows may require different rarefaction indicators or more accurate kinetic branches. In particular, accurate heat-flux prediction may require a more detailed kinetic branch than the present slip/jump or half-range Maxwellian approximation. The present framework is modular and can be combined with more sophisticated continuum fluxes, kinetic fluxes, or DVM/DSMC-informed closures. 

# 14 Conclusions 

A log-Gaussian scale-space limiter has been proposed for hybrid continuum–ballistic gas dynam-ics. By treating the local Knudsen number as a stochastic scale ratio, the continuum and ballistic weights are defined as complementary cumulative probabilities in logarithmic Knudsen space. The resulting weights possess correct limiting behavior in the continuum and free-molecular regimes. The limiter is incorporated into a conservative interface flux blending continuum and kinetic contributions. Numerical experiments using planar Fourier and Couette DVM/BGK references show that rarefaction corrections improve the profiles relative to NSF. Calibration against these references suggests K0 = 0 .03 , σ = 2 .5, reducing the combined mean profile error by about 40% 

for the tested planar wall-bounded flows. The present study is a proof of concept and calibration study rather than a replacement for kinetic solvers. Future work will include DSMC and UGKS comparisons, normal shock structures, multidimensional hypersonic flows, improved non-Maxwellian kinetic branches, and analysis of positivity, entropy stability, and asymptotic-preserving behavior.
